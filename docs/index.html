How RLMs are Different — Kevin Madura, AlixPartners｜AI Engineer

In a podcast appearance, AlixPartners' Kevin Madura explains why recursive/loop methods beat both naive prompting and agentic tool-calling: the context window stays small because subtasks are deferred to sub-models and inputs live as REPL variables rather than attended tokens, sidestepping the 'dumb zone' of context rot. He traces loop engineering to the Ralph Wiggum technique, born in June 2025 when Jeff Huntley spent six weeks and $6,000 running Claude Sonnet autonomously to build a compiler for a new programming language. The practical framing is the Research-Plan-Implement workflow: parallel research sub-agents condense a codebase, a human reviews the plan, then a fresh context executes.

Emergent Adversarial Robustness from Oscillatory Predictive Learning

This arXiv paper introduces Oscillatory Predictive Learning (OPL), a training scheme whose adversarial robustness emerges from the architecture rather than from adversarial training. The method ties two network branches together with a cosine-similarity objective (L_Cos) computed over stop-gradient representations. Evaluations use l-infinity perturbations at epsilon of 8/255 on CIFAR-10/100, with RobustBench numbers cited only as context because those follow the standard AutoAttack protocol rather than OPL's randomized setting. The notable claim is that robustness can be a byproduct of predictive, oscillatory dynamics.
