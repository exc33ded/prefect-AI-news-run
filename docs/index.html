On-premise medical AI agents for reliable clinical decision-making

A Nature paper describes on-premise medical AI agents for reliable clinical decision-making, tackling how uncertainty propagates across multi-step agentic workflows. The authors introduce ConsistencyDx and ConsistencyR, which operate on full-output embeddings — rather than discrete answer clusters — and are applied separately to diagnostic conclusions and reasoning traces, distinguishing them from self-consistency and semantic entropy methods. They also assess the sensitivity of Consistency to the number of runs (N), embedding-model choice and decoding temperature. The work matters because most prior uncertainty work targets single-turn QA or chain-of-thought reasoning, not iterative tool use.

LLM-STATS
K-Bench: a clinically calibrated benchmark for evaluating large ...

K-Bench (arXiv:2609.15555v1) is a clinically calibrated benchmark for evaluating conversational AI in mental-health contexts. Conversations are generated as patient-agent and target-LLM interactions of up to 20 turns, with clinician-calibrated scoring and detailed rubric items covering patient disclosure, realism and emotional believability. To limit contamination, the exact prompt text, turn-level disclosure rules, lexical trigger lists, phrase banks and deterministic option pools are withheld. The authors describe it as a new standard for clinical risk coverage and large-scale configuration comparison.
